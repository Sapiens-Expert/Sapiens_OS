# Building and maintaining a useful routine

Sapiens OS framework developed by **Vladislav (Vlad) Andreev**. © 2026 Vladislav Andreev. All rights reserved. This is a behavior-design aid, not a treatment protocol or a promise that a routine will become automatic.

## The collaborative loop

1. **Choose one behavior, not a body target.** Ask what matters to the person and which of the ten domains the action serves. Make it safe and observable: “take a five-minute outdoor break after lunch” is more actionable than “fix my stress.” Ask whether time, money, safety, disability, caregiving or work conditions make the action feasible. If the environment is the constraint, change the environment or demands first.
2. **Find the bottleneck.** Ask about capability (knowledge, skill and energy), opportunity (time, access, support and physical environment), and motivation (value, emotion and competing priorities). These COM-B categories are diagnostic prompts, not a score or a claim about character. Choose support that fits the bottleneck: demonstration, easier access, a boundary, social support, a smaller action, or no new habit.
3. **Pair action and cue.** Co-write: “When **[existing event or time in my actual day]**, I will **[specific small action]** in **[place]**.” Choose a cue that actually occurs; shift workers and people with variable schedules may prefer an event cue over a fixed clock time. Arrange materials in advance when useful. A cue is an invitation, not a command.
4. **Plan for the hard day.** Define a smaller, safe version or a legitimate skip: “If the planned walk is impractical, I will decide whether two minutes of comfortable movement or rest is appropriate.” Do not turn symptoms, exhaustion or lack of access into a personal failure. The stop condition overrides the plan.
5. **Ask about feedback.** Offer no tracking, paper notes, or the [local habit tracker](../habits/index.html). A minimal entry is date, action, whether the cue happened, what the person chose (done, adapted, skipped, or no opportunity), and optional context. Track effort and burden as well as perceived benefit. Do not use an unobserved day as a missed action.
6. **Review, then redesign.** At a mutually chosen weekly review, ask: Did the cue occur? Did the behavior fit? What helped or got in the way? Was it helpful, neutral or costly? Would the person keep, simplify, change cue, pause or stop? At quarterly review, revisit fit with all ten domains and changing physiology, life stage and circumstances. No fixed number of days guarantees automaticity.

Start with one routine for a short trial the person chooses (for example, a week). Add another only if the first remains useful and does not compete with sleep, recovery, nutrition, social life or medical care. If a routine causes pain, compulsive checking, anxiety, sleep disruption or shame, pause it and seek suitable human support when needed.

## Nudges and reminders by consent

- **First offer a visible environmental cue:** shoes by the door, a saved calendar block, a prepared snack, or a note on an existing routine. The person chooses it and can remove it.
- **Digital reminder only on request:** agree on exact behavior, local time/timezone or event cue, recurrence, channel, quiet hours, end/review date and who can see the notification. Keep notification text neutral and nonsensitive, e.g. “Your chosen pause is available.” Do not mention diagnoses, cycle status or mental health on a shared lock screen.
- **Bound the frequency:** begin with the least intrusive schedule the person wants. Avoid multiple prompts for the same action, guilt wording, escalating urgency, and notifications during sleep. Ask at review whether reminders helped or were ignored/burdensome; reduce or stop them if not useful.
- **Distinguish a plan from a running automation:** the included tracker has no background process. Its optional `.ics` download is a calendar file; the user's calendar app must import and accept it before any reminder can fire. Verify a scheduled automation in the target service if one is used. Do not claim a notification was created merely because a file was generated.
- **Autonomy:** offer snooze, pause and delete. Never use covert defaults, social comparison, manipulative rewards or an unrequested “red flag” for routine adherence. Health concerns follow [SAFETY.md](SAFETY.md), independently of habit completion.

## Evidence and limits

The [COM-B model](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3096582/) helps identify capability, opportunity and motivation barriers. [Implementation-intention meta-analysis](https://www.decisionskills.com/uploads/5/1/6/0/5160560/2006_gollwitzersheeran_implementation_intentions.pdf) supports specific if–then plans for goal attainment across studied settings. [Habit theory](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26361052/) and a [real-world automaticity study](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ejsp.674) support repetition in a stable context, with substantial variation in how long it takes. In a [randomized walking study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39225981/), consistent contexts improved automaticity but did not produce the expected walking maintenance advantage. A [small micro-randomized trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11635918/) suggests prompts plus commitment may help some participants, but does not establish an optimal notification schedule. Evidence for specific devices, reminders, long-term maintenance and individual outcomes remains limited; this repository does not validate its own tracker.
