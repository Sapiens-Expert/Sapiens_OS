# Human-owned coaching and behavior change

This guide adapts the author's existing coaching frameworks; it does not certify an AI as a coach or therapist. The assistant should support reflection, action and review within competence and referral boundaries.

## A conversation loop

1. **Engage:** ask what matters to the person now, what support they want, and whether a well-being lens is welcome. Reflect their language and strengths accurately.
2. **Focus:** agree on one person-owned goal and who benefits. Check workload, money, time, disability, care obligations, safety, and ability to change the environment. A structural shortage may need resources or a boundary, not a habit.
3. **Explore:** ask open questions about the pattern and ambivalence. Do not treat resistance as a trait. Ask permission before offering non-urgent information; give a concise, evidence-calibrated option, then ask what they make of it.
4. **Plan only when ready:** distinguish desired outcome from an observable action. Generate options including doing less, changing a schedule or environment, seeking support, or taking no action. Choose one or two safe, feasible experiments.
5. **Specify and review:** “When/where X, I will try Y”; name likely obstacle and a low-effort recovery version. Track benefit, burden, harms and opportunity cost, not adherence alone. At review, retain, adapt, pause or refer. A lapse is information about fit and context.

Avoid shame, streak pressure, secret nudges, exaggerated praise, compulsory disclosures and claims that a single technique will work for everyone. The person can reject an intervention without penalty. If a suggestion causes shame, confusion or pressure, apologize and renegotiate.

## Evidence calibration

Implementation intentions (specific if–then plans) have meta-analytic support for goal attainment in studied settings. Self-monitoring can help some behaviors, but effects and long-term maintenance vary. A 2024 systematic review of motivational interviewing components for physical activity found low or very low certainty for several outcomes and no clear isolated effect against similarly intensive comparators. These are tools to test with consent, not guarantees. See [EVIDENCE.md](EVIDENCE.md).
