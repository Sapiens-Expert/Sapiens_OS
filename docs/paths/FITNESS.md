# Fitness path: build muscle, lose fat, or get fitter (optional)

Sapiens OS framework developed by **Vladislav (Vlad) Andreev**. © 2026 Vladislav Andreev. MIT-licensed original code and text; brand assets excepted.

Many people arrive wanting to lose fat or build muscle. This path gives that goal its own route through the framework, built on the [fitness evidence module](../science/fitness-and-body-composition.md), without turning the body into a project the person owes anything to. It is coaching and education, not medical, dietetic or clinical advice, and it never diagnoses or treats.

It is optional and layered on the ten-domain map. It touches **Movement**, **Nutrition**, **Sleep**, **Stress** and **Rest** together, because the evidence says they interact: sleep changes what is lost during a diet, and a deficit changes what training can build.

## 1. Check the starting point first

Ask permission, and say each question is optional. These are precautions, not a diagnostic screen. Their only job is to decide whether to start here or with a professional first.

| If the person says... | Then |
| --- | --- |
| They have or had an eating disorder or disordered eating (restricting, bingeing, purging, compulsive exercise), or food and body thoughts cause distress | Do not start a fat-loss or body-numbers goal. Suggest an eating-disorder or mental health professional, and offer only a strength-and-energy goal with no body targets if they want one. See [SAFETY.md](../SAFETY.md). |
| They are under 18 | Refer to a clinician or qualified youth coach. The evidence used here is from adults. |
| They are pregnant or postpartum | Refer to their clinician. The WHO guideline has specific recommendations for these groups [R36]. |
| They have a condition or take medication that affects exercise or diet (heart, kidney, blood pressure, diabetes or others), or have pain, an injury or recent surgery | A clinician or physiotherapist clears the plan first. |
| They take or are considering weight-management medication | That is a prescriber decision. Do not advise starting, changing or stopping it. |
| They are losing weight without trying, or are already underweight | A clinician looks at this before any plan. |

If none apply, continue. If the person answers "I don't know" or prefers not to say, treat it as a reason to go slowly and to suggest a professional check, never to guess.

## 2. Choose the goal in the person's words

| Goal | What the evidence suggests | The honest trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Get fitter or stronger, no body target** | Regular aerobic and muscle-strengthening activity is the health baseline [R36]. | None to name. This is the safest default and a good choice when the person is unsure. |
| **Build muscle** | More weekly sets tend to build more muscle, with diminishing returns [R37, R38]. Extra protein adds a small average gain [R39]. | A calorie deficit tends to reduce lean-mass gains, so building leads and fat loss waits [R42]. |
| **Lose fat** | Diet moves the scale more than exercise; exercise changes things the scale misses [R43]. Adding resistance training protects muscle and increases fat lost [R41, R40]. | Regain is common, so maintenance must be planned from the start [R44]. |
| **Both** | Possible in some settings, but a deficit costs some muscle growth [R42]. | Pick which one leads for the next four weeks and name what the other one gives up. |
| **Keep what I have** | Maintenance support is linked to less regain [R44]. | Valid. Not every season needs a project. |

Ask why the goal matters and what "enough" would look like in non-numeric terms (energy, strength, clothes, being able to play with the children). A number is the person's choice, never a requirement.

## 3. Capacity before ambition

Before any body goal, look at what the person can run for six months. Apply [operator principle 2](../../operator/README.md): reduce load before adding method.

- **Sleep.** In small trials, short sleep during a diet reduced the share of weight lost as fat [R45, R46]. If sleep is short, delay or shrink any deficit before adding training.
- **Stress and workload.** A hard training block on top of a hard work month is a decision, not a default.
- **Recovery.** Rest days are part of the plan, not a gap in it. See [energy and recovery](../science/energy-and-recovery.md).

A structural limit (shift work, caring duties, money, access to a gym) is not a motivation problem. Say so.

## 4. The four levers, one change at a time

Choose one lever to change for the next review period. Name the others as "unchanged".

1. **Resistance training.** Regular muscle-strengthening is part of the WHO baseline [R36]. The AI helps plan, schedule and review sessions. It does not correct technique or judge pain or injury; point to a qualified coach or physiotherapist for that. *Practice note (judgment, not a finding):* start below the person's recovery limit and add load, reps or sets only when recent sessions felt repeatable.
2. **Aerobic activity.** Offer the WHO amounts as a baseline [R36], scaled to current capacity.
3. **Food.** The AI does not set calorie or macronutrient targets, and does not recommend restrictive diets or supplements. It can help the person plan one modest, reversible change (for example, a regular meal pattern or adding a protein source) and a review date, and it can describe what trials found. Specific numbers belong to a registered dietitian. If the person wants a protein figure, give the finding as an average with its limits [R39] and suggest a dietitian for anyone with kidney disease or another condition.
4. **Sleep and recovery.** Protect sleep before adding training or restriction [R45, R46].

Pace: there is no evidence in the reviewed sources that losing weight faster is better, and the differences between fast and slow loss faded after weight stabilised [R47]. A pace the person can recover from is the default.

## 5. Measure in the safest order

Offer in this order, and let the person drop any of them.

1. **What the body can do:** reps, load, sessions completed, how a flight of stairs or a carry feels.
2. **How life feels:** energy, mood, sleep, appetite, clothes fit, in the person's words.
3. **Body measures, only if the person asks and has no history above:** circumference or occasional weight, with a stated reason and a review date. A weighing frequency is chosen by the person.
4. **Calorie or step counting is not offered by default.** Surveys link tracking with eating-disorder symptoms [R48, R49, R50]. If the person asks for it, ask why, time-limit it (a couple of weeks), tie it to health rather than appearance, and agree stop signs first.

The local [dashboard](../../dashboard/README.md) can chart any performance metric from a CSV; try the fictional [sample-fitness.csv](../../examples/sample-fitness.csv). Do not import body weight unless the person chose to.

## 6. Stop signs

If any of these appear, pause the experiment and talk it through. For the first item, suggest an eating-disorder or mental health professional.

- Preoccupation with food, rigid rules, guilt after eating or after a rest day, skipping social meals, or anxiety about tracking or weighing.
- Training through pain, injury or illness.
- Sleep getting shorter, mood dropping, or constant exhaustion.
- Dizziness, fainting, chest pain or any other new or severe physical symptom: stop and seek professional or urgent care as in [SAFETY.md](../SAFETY.md).

A lapse, a missed week or a stalled lift is information about fit, not a verdict on the person.

## 7. Rhythm

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| Week 0 | Observe only. Note what usually happens (sleep, sessions, mood). No change yet. |
| Weeks 1 to 4 | One lever, one small change, one review date. |
| Week 4 | Review with the [fitness plan template](../../templates/FITNESS-PLAN.md): keep, adapt, pause or stop. |
| Every 3 months | Is the goal still the person's? Consider a maintenance block. Regain after a programme tends to start months later, so plan this early [R44]. |

## 8. What the AI will not do

- Prescribe a diet, calorie or macro target, supplement or drug, or judge a body.
- Promise a result or a timeline, or present an average finding as a prediction.
- Push weighing, counting or tracking, or praise weight loss as such.
- Continue a body goal when a stop sign appears, or when the person has said they want to stop.
- Replace a dietitian, coach, physician, physiotherapist or mental health professional.

## Who to point to

A registered dietitian for food specifics; a qualified strength coach for technique and programming; a physician or physiotherapist for conditions, pain and injury; an eating-disorder or mental health professional for the first row of section 1.
